# A metric that cannot fail is noise

SLOP2875 · Week 9

---

## For eight weeks the instrument has been right

This week is about how it goes wrong without going red.

---

## The claim

A measurement saturates when everything you point it at gets the same score.

It still prints numbers. It still passes its tests. It still looks like evidence.

The only way to catch it: has it ever told two things apart?

---

## A ceiling

The first simulated player had no reaction time.

15 wins out of 15, with every build it was given.

Including builds that were doing nothing.

Every card looked equally good because nothing could look bad.

---

## A floor

Extra damage, refuse every other card. Median survival across six levels:

66.0 s, 66.0 s, 67.6 s, 62.7 s, 64.4 s, 65.1 s.

The first boss wall stands at 66 s. Every build dies against it, and the dial is connected to nothing the metric can see.

---

## A cap

Kills in the first 38 s, median of 51 runs. Nine different builds:

34, 34, 34, 34, 34, 34, 34, 34, 34.

The game spawns about that many enemies in 38 s. Every build kills all of them.

The metric is measuring the spawn rate.

# A test that cannot fail is noise

---

## What was done about it

The test comparing cards by survival time had caught three dead cards, once.

After the boss wall went in, it could not fail any more.

It was deleted, with the reason written down.

## Reading, weeks 9 and 10

Gudmundsson and colleagues, Human-Like Playtesting with Deep Learning.

Two bots predicting how hard a new Candy Crush level is. The one that played like a person predicted better.

Read it before next week, when you become the person.

---

## Thursday

For the metric you have relied on most: its spread between your best and worst build,

beside its spread between two different sets of 51 seeds.

If the first is not clearly bigger, you have been reading noise.
