# One run tells you nothing

SLOP2875 · Week 4

---

{/* _class: quote */}

## Seed 97 ends at 69.3 s

That sentence is true.

It is an anecdote.

---

## The claim

A single run is one draw from a distribution.

The distribution is the thing you are trying to talk about.

---

## How balance arguments go wrong

Quote one run. Or the best run. Or the run you happened to watch.

Everybody in the argument is being honest.

---

## How wide it is

The 150 ms player. Ordinary upgrade choices. 501 different seeds.

Shortest run: 29.8 s.

Longest run: the full 120 s.

Nothing differs between them except the dice.

---

## The median as the runs grow

| Runs | Median survival |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 69.3 s |
| 5 | 69.3 s |
| 15 | 81.5 s |
| 51 | 78.2 s |
| 101 | 78.0 s |
| 501 | 73.7 s |

---

## It never stops moving completely

Fifteen runs put the median 12 s above what one run said.

Five hundred and one pull it back down by 8 s.

What you decide is how much movement the decision in front of you can tolerate.

# 51

---

## Why 51

It is odd, so the median is a run that actually happened.

51 runs of the specimen cost 166 ms.

There is no excuse for quoting fewer.

---

## Thursday

The smallest number of runs at which your own median stops moving by more than 1 s.

One sentence on why you would, or would not, trust a claim made on fewer.
