## Before the tutorial

Read Jaffe and colleagues up to the end of their definition of restricted
play. Write down which card you think is worth the most. Seal it.

## In the tutorial

Baseline first: your delayed player turned down to 100 ms, leftmost card every
time, 51 seeds. At 150 ms it wins 2 of 51, which leaves a ban almost nothing to
take away. Then seven restricted runs. The only change each time is the card your player is
forbidden to take.

Sort the table by wins lost. Open what you sealed.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Fifteen runs.** At 15 runs the baseline is 3 wins and the seven banned rows
  are 2, 2, 1, 0, 3, 2, 3. Every row is within three wins of every other and
  the table ranks nothing. At 51 runs the same rows are 7, 7, 3, 1, 9, 7, 10
  against a baseline of 12, and pickup reach is plainly worth the most.
- **A baseline from somewhere else.** The baseline has to be the same player, on
  the same seeds, on the same day. A remembered 12 from last week, after you
  have edited the policy, is a different experiment.
- **A ban that changes the player.** When the banned card is offered, take the
  next one along. Do not skip the level-up: a player who takes nothing that
  round is weaker for a second reason, and you cannot separate the two.

## What a finished answer looks like

> Baseline 12 of 51. Most costly ban: pickup reach, 1 of 51, 11 lost. Least:
> shockwave, 10 of 51, 2 lost. I had sealed "damage". It lost 5.
