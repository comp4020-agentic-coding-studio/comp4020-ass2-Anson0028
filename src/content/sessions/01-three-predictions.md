## Before the tutorial

Read `rules.ts` from top to bottom. It is about 800 lines and it is the whole
game. Do not run it and do not look for a playable build.

## In the tutorial

The first 20 minutes are in pairs. One person states an opinion about the game
from the rules alone ("the shockwave looks weak", "the second boss comes too
soon"). The other refuses to accept it until it has a number, a unit and a run
count in it. Then swap.

The rest of the time you write your own three and put them on the wall. The
room votes on which prediction is most likely to be wrong. Keep that one; it is
the first thing you test in week three.

## Where it goes wrong

Three ways, and the room will do all three in the first ten minutes.

- **No run count.** "A player who skips upgrades survives past 66 s" cannot be
  checked. Once? Always? "In at least 1 run out of 15" can.
- **A prediction that cannot lose.** "Sometimes the second boss is too hard" is
  true of every game ever made. If no result would embarrass you, it is still
  an opinion.
- **A unit that hides the claim.** "Survives longer" is a direction. "Median
  survival rises by more than 5 s over 51 runs" is a size.

## What a finished answer looks like

> Banning the shockwave costs an ordinary player fewer than 3 wins out of 51.
> Wrong if it costs 3 or more.

One sentence for the claim, one for what would prove it wrong. Week 6 measures
this one.

## Afterwards

Your three predictions are your answers on the first quiz. Nothing else is
marked this week.
