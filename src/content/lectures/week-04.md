Seed 97 ends at 69.3 s. That sentence is true, and it is an anecdote.

## The claim

A single run is one draw from a distribution, and the distribution is the thing
you are trying to talk about. Quoting one run, or the best run, or the run you
happened to watch, is how balance arguments go wrong while everybody in them is
being honest.

## How wide it is

The 150 ms player from last week, ordinary upgrade choices, 501 different
seeds. The shortest run ends at 29.8 s. The longest reaches the full 120 s.
Nothing differs between them except the dice.

Here is the median as the number of runs grows:

| Runs | Median survival |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 69.3 s |
| 5 | 69.3 s |
| 15 | 81.5 s |
| 51 | 78.2 s |
| 101 | 78.0 s |
| 501 | 73.7 s |

Fifteen runs put the median 12 s above what one run said. Five hundred and one
pull it back down by 8 s. It never stops moving completely. What you get to
decide is how much movement the decision in front of you can tolerate.

This course uses 51 as its working number. It is odd, so the median is a run
that actually happened, and 51 runs of the specimen cost 166 ms. There is no
excuse for quoting fewer.

## What you leave with

The smallest number of runs at which your own median stops moving by more than
1 s, and one sentence on why you would or would not trust a claim made on
fewer.
