[Here it is.](https://comp4020-agentic-coding-studio.github.io/comp4020-crit5-Anson0028/)
You have measured this game for nine weeks. Go and play it.

## The claim

Simulation answers the questions you thought to ask. Playing asks the ones you
did not. Neither is the backup for the other, and a balance process that has
only one of them has a blind side it cannot see from inside.

## Three things the specimen's simulation never saw

**Standing still killed nothing.** The opening weapon used to be a shard
circling the player. In the live game, standing still for six seconds killed
no enemies at all: the shard was a point, and it hit about one enemy in five.
Every simulated run was full of kills, because a simulated player is always
moving, and a moving player drags the shard across things
([`bfee9c7`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit5-Anson0028/commit/bfee9c7)).

**31 kills, level three.** A person playing for 45 s killed 31 enemies and was
still on level three. The experience was all there, 43 pieces on the field,
and the player walked past nearly all of it. The simulated player steers onto
a three-pixel dot without effort. A person does not
([`4c419ca`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit5-Anson0028/commit/4c419ca)).

**Nobody knew what the bar was for.** The run timer was a thin pale line along
the top. Someone who had played the game asked what it was
([`8c17b35`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-crit5-Anson0028/commit/8c17b35)).
No simulated player has ever been confused by an interface.

## Why this week is here and not first

If you had played in week one, you would have spent nine weeks defending your
first impression. Now you have predictions on paper, and the game gets to
surprise you in a way you can write down.

## What you leave with

A list of what ten minutes of play showed you that your simulation could not
have, and one item from it turned into something your instrument now checks.
