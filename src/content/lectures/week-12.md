You have spent eleven weeks learning to distrust a number. This week you
commit to one.

## The claim

A balance change is finished when someone else can check it. "It feels better
now" is where this course started, and it is not allowed at the end.

## What a defence contains

One constant in the specimen, its old value and its new one. Then, in this
order, each taking a minute or less:

1. **The prediction** you made before measuring, with its number and unit.
2. **The instrument**: which simulated players, what reaction times, and that
   it was 51 runs on fixed seeds.
3. **The result**, as a distribution and not a single figure: wins out of 51
   and median survival, before and after.
4. **What you took away**: a restricted-play comparison showing the change did
   not quietly kill an option.
5. **Who tried to break it**: the lazy strategies from week eight, run again
   against the new value.
6. **Where the instrument could be lying**: the ceiling, floor or cap nearest
   to your metric, and why you are clear of it.
7. **What you felt** when you played the changed game, and whether it agrees.

If any of the seven is missing, that is the first question you will be asked.

## What you leave with

The number.
